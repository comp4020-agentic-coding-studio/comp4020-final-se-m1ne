# Crit 9 Reflection

## 1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The biggest breakthrough was realising that real-time collaboration is not just about making the final drawing appear in another browser. At first, the other person could only see a completed line after I released the mouse. Even though the update was automatic, it did not really feel like we were drawing together.

I changed this so that other people could see the line while it was being drawn. I later found the same problem with the eraser: the other browser showed a grey trail instead of the artwork actually disappearing. Fixing that made the shared drawing experience feel much more natural.

Another important part of this week was recovering from my pixel-art experiment. I originally chose pixel art because I thought it would be easier than building a more complete drawing application. However, problems with grid alignment, freezing brushes and slow drawing became difficult to fix. Returning to freehand drawing was frustrating after spending so much time on pixel art, but it allowed me to focus on making the basic experience work properly.

## 2. What did this work change about who I am as a developer?

This week made me more willing to abandon an idea when it is not working, even after spending a lot of time on it. I used to think that choosing a simpler-looking direction would automatically make development easier. The pixel-art experiment showed me that this is not always true.

I also became more careful about accepting Claude Code's results. A feature can pass tests and still feel wrong when I actually use it. I now try to test changes with two browsers, watch what happens during drawing rather than only afterwards, and explain the specific problem before asking Claude to fix it.

I think I am becoming less focused on adding features and more focused on whether the website actually works well for the people using it.
